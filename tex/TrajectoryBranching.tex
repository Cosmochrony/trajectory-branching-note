\author{J\'er\^ome Beau}

\begin{abstract}
The de Sitter solution shows that accelerated expansion can be a property of empty geometry rather than a
consequence of matter content.
This note identifies the projective analogue of that statement inside the Heisenberg spectral admissibility
cascade.
The result does not claim that Heisenberg balls grow exponentially.
They do not: by the Bass--Guivarc'h formula their growth is polynomial of degree four.
The exponential component survives only after replacing endpoint counting by projectively distinguishable
trajectory counting.
We define a hierarchy of trajectory distinguishability notions and show that any endpoint-based definition
collapses to polynomial growth.
The canonical O12-compatible residual channel is $b$-only and therefore bounded by the non-backtracking
abelian-shadow entropy.
For a generic probe, the residual profile separates the $b$-sequences on the pre-saturation window.
Consequently, the distinguishable-history count is the Pell count
\[
\Nb(n)=2\Nb(n-1)+\Nb(n-2),
\qquad
\hb=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\log \Nb(n)=\log(1+\sqrt{2}).
\]
The equality is witnessed exactly on the sampled histories of a multi-prime campaign
($q\in\{29,61,101,211\}$) and the genericity condition on the probe is proved to fail only on a finite union
of proper real-algebraic hypersurfaces.
The full-history Gabor channel is strictly compressive: a geodesic-death mechanism confines nonzero symbols
to trajectories whose abelian shadow is an outward geodesic, and for a generic probe its
distinguishable-history count is exactly $2^{n+2}-4n-1$ on the pre-saturation window, so the channel has
positive and exactly pinned symbolic entropy $\log 2$, strictly below the canonical rate.
The last separation step behind the exact count --- mirror pairs at equal $|b|$ --- is closed by proving
the generic non-vanishing of a mixed two-anchor coefficient, through a Hermitian jet at the orthogonal
point of an anchor moment domain.
Its finite-horizon compression profile is retained as the candidate input for an evolving effective equation
of state.
Structural reading (interpretation, not a derived result): accelerated expansion, if inherited by the
cosmological branch, is not driven by exponential growth of Heisenberg states but by exponential branching
of projectively distinguishable histories, and the branching skeleton of that history space is now an
exactly enumerated combinatorial object.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} non-injective projection; Heisenberg group; spectral admissibility;
trajectory branching; projection entropy; de Sitter expansion; Pell numbers; full-spark Gabor systems;
dark energy.

\section{Motivation}
\label{sec:motivation}

The historical de Sitter solution~\cite{deSitter1917} establishes that accelerated expansion need not be
sourced by ordinary matter, and the Milne model~\cite{Milne1935} shows that even a linearly coasting empty
universe is not trivially excluded by expansion data.
In the projective setting considered here, the analogous question is whether accelerated expansion can arise
from the growth of admissible projection histories rather than from a material dark-energy component.

The issue is non-trivial because the spectral admissibility programme moved from LPS expander
graphs~\cite{Lubotzky1988,Beau2026a5} to the Heisenberg Cayley graph
$\Heis(\Zq)$~\cite{Beau2026a16,Beau2026a20}.
LPS balls have exponential growth, whereas Heisenberg balls have polynomial growth.
Therefore the two-regime expansion mechanism proposed in the relaxation paper~\cite{Beau2026a5} --- polynomial
shell growth followed by exponential branching of admissible trajectories --- cannot be transferred by
counting endpoints.
The only possible surviving exponential component must live in the redundancy of histories over endpoints.

This note isolates that component, proves where it can and cannot reside, pins its rate in the canonical
channel and in the full-history channel, and states precisely what is and is not established for the
cosmological branch~\cite{Beau2026d,Cosmochrony}.

\section{Endpoint Growth and the Heisenberg Obstruction}
\label{sec:endpoint}

\subsection{Polynomial endpoint growth}
\label{sec:bass-guivarch}

Let $B(n)$ denote the ball of radius $n$ in the discrete Heisenberg group with generating set
$S=\{X^{\pm1},Y^{\pm1}\}$.
By the Bass--Guivarc'h formula~\cite{Bass1972,Guivarch1973}, the homogeneous growth degree of the integer
Heisenberg group is
\[
D = 1+1+2\cdot 1 = 4 ,
\]
so $|B(n)| \asymp n^4$ before finite-$q$ saturation, and $|B(n)|$ saturates at $q^3$ on $\Heis(\Zq)$.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint obstruction]
\label{prop:endpoint-obstruction}
Any distinguishability relation depending only on the endpoint in $\Heis(\Zq)$ has polynomial pre-saturation
growth and finite-$q$ saturation.
It cannot support an asymptotic de Sitter-like exponential rate.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Any endpoint-based invariant factors through the Heisenberg element $(a,b,\gamma)$ reached at time $n$.
The number of reachable values is bounded by the ball volume, which has polynomial Bass--Guivarc'h growth of
degree $4$ and saturates at $q^3$.
Both bounds have zero exponential rate.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Why the central fibre is not enough}
\label{sec:central-fibre}

The central coordinate $\gamma$ records the oriented area swept by the horizontal projection of the path.
This is a genuine history integral --- the level-two term of the Chen signature of the path~\cite{Chen1957}
--- but it is the only one the group retains.
A step-two nilpotent endpoint truncates the signature at level two: iterated path information of order three
or higher is erased by the group structure itself.
Counting the central register therefore does not change the growth class: the degree $4$ already decomposes
as $2$ (abelian displacement) $+\,2$ (area values $\asymp n^2$).

\begin{corollary}[Endpoint no-go]
\label{cor:endpoint-nogo}
Any invariant that is a function of $(a,b,\gamma)$ has zero exponential distinguishability rate before
finite-$q$ saturation.
\end{corollary}

\section{Trajectory Distinguishability}
\label{sec:distinguishability}

Throughout, a trajectory is a non-backtracking word $\gamma=(g_1,\dots,g_n)\in S^n$, $g_{k+1}\neq g_k^{-1}$,
identified with the path $u_0=e$, $u_k=u_{k-1}g_k$ in $\Heis(\Zq)$.
The number of such words is $4\cdot 3^{\,n-1}$, so the unconstrained history entropy rate is $\log 3$.

\begin{definition}[D1: endpoint distinguishability]
\label{def:d1}
Two trajectories are D1-distinguishable if their final Heisenberg endpoints are distinct.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[D2: fibre-register distinguishability]
\label{def:d2}
Two trajectories are D2-distinguishable if their final abelian displacement or their final central register
are distinct.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[D3: endpoint-functional distinguishability]
\label{def:d3}
Two trajectories are D3-distinguishable if some fixed function of their final Heisenberg endpoint separates
them.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[D4: projective trajectory distinguishability]
\label{def:d4}
Two trajectories $\gamma,\gamma'$ of length $n$ are D4-distinguishable if their projected trajectory profiles
differ,
\[
\PiProj(U_n(\gamma))\neq \PiProj(U_n(\gamma')) ,
\]
where $U_n(\gamma)$ is the sequence of projective data accumulated along the path, not only the endpoint.
\end{definition}

D1 and D2 are endpoint notions and D2 $=$ D1 on $\Heis(\Zq)$ by Section~\ref{sec:central-fibre}.
D3 is the full endpoint no-go class of Corollary~\ref{cor:endpoint-nogo}.
D4 is the only class capable of retaining exponential history branching, and the remainder of the note
determines how much of the $\log 3$ word entropy a concrete projective profile retains.

\section{The Canonical O12 Residual Channel}
\label{sec:canonical-channel}

\subsection{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

Fix a prime $q$ and a central character $c\neq 0$.
The O12 pipeline~\cite{Beau2026a16} acts with the exact Weil representation $\rho_c$ of $\Heis(\Zq)$ on
$\mathbb{C}^q$,
\[
[\rho_c(a,b,\gamma)\varphi](x) = \psi_c\!\bigl(\gamma+b(x-a)\bigr)\,\varphi(x-a),
\qquad
\psi_c(t)=e^{2\pi i c t/q},
\]
builds fingerprint vectors over BFS shells, and accumulates their Gram--Schmidt span.
The D4 profile of a trajectory used here is the sequence of residual norms of a probe vector
$\rho_c(u_k)\,\varphi_0$ against the span frozen at the end of shell $k-1$, together with the conjugate
factor at $q-c$ following the pair-observable protocol~\cite{Beau2026a20,Beau2026a29}.
The reference state $\varphi_0$ (the probe) is either the uniform state (the O12 default) or a fixed generic
unit vector.

\subsection{The character lemma and the $b$-only theorem}
\label{sec:b-only}

\begin{lemma}[Character structure of the O12 span]
\label{lem:character}
Every Weil vector on the uniform state, and every O12 fingerprint vector, has exactly one nonzero Fourier
coefficient.
Explicitly, $\rho_c(a,b,\gamma)\,|\mathrm{unif}\rangle$ is a global phase times the additive character
$e_{cb}$, and a fingerprint built from probe points with $b$-coordinates $b_1,b_2,b_3$ and characters
$c_1,c_2,c_3$ is a global phase times $e_{c_1b_1+c_2b_2+c_3b_3}$.
Consequently the O12 span is a coordinate subspace in the Fourier basis: at the end of shell $k$ it equals
$\mathrm{span}\{e_m : m\in F_k\}$ for a deterministic frequency set $F_k\subseteq\Zq$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
On the uniform state, $[\rho_c(a,b,\gamma)\,\mathrm{unif}](x) = q^{-1/2}\,\psi_c(\gamma-ab)\,e^{2\pi i cbx/q}$,
a phase times $e_{cb}$.
A fingerprint is a pointwise product of three such vectors, hence a phase times the character at the sum
frequency.
Characters are exactly orthogonal, so the accumulated Gram--Schmidt span is the span of the characters hit,
independently of the enumeration order within shells.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[$b$-only residual channel]
\label{thm:b-only}
For any probe $\varphi_0$, the residual norm of $\rho_c(a,b,\gamma)\,\varphi_0$ against the frozen O12 span
at the end of shell $k-1$ equals
\[
r_k(b)^2 \;=\; \sum_{m\notin F_{k-1}} \bigl|\widehat{\varphi_0}(m-cb)\bigr|^2 .
\]
It is invariant under translation in $a$ and under the central phase $\gamma$, and depends on the path point
only through $b$.
The mono-path profile of the canonical channel therefore factors through the $b$-shadow
$(b_1,\dots,b_n)$ of the trajectory.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In the Fourier basis, modulation by $b$ shifts frequencies by $cb$, translation by $a$ multiplies the $m$-th
coefficient by a phase, and $\gamma$ contributes a global phase:
$\widehat{\rho_c(a,b,\gamma)\varphi_0}(m) = e^{i\theta(a,\gamma,m)}\,\widehat{\varphi_0}(m-cb)$.
The residual against a coordinate subspace $\mathrm{span}\{e_m:m\in F_{k-1}\}$ is the coefficient mass on the
complement, which discards all phases.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Uniform-probe collapse; erased fibre]
\label{cor:uniform-collapse}
With the uniform probe, $r_k(b)\in\{0,1\}$ according to whether $cb\in F_{k-1}$, and the channel collapses to
a binary function of $b$.
Moreover, for any probe and any irreducible representation, the central charge acts as a global phase, so
$\gamma$ is unconditionally erased from every mono-path norm profile.
The erased fibre of the canonical D4 channel is exactly the central register together with the sign data of
the $X$-steps.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Pell counting of $b$-shadows}
\label{sec:pell}

\begin{proposition}[Pell count]
\label{prop:pell}
Let $\Nb(n)$ be the number of $b$-increment sequences realised by non-backtracking trajectories of length
$n$.
Then $\Nb(1)=3$, $\Nb(2)=7$, and
\[
\Nb(n)=2\Nb(n-1)+\Nb(n-2) \qquad (n\ge 3),
\]
so $\Nb(n)=3,7,17,41,99,239,577,1393,3363,\dots$ (the numerators of the continued-fraction convergents of
$\sqrt2$~\cite{NSW1981}), and
\[
\hb=\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac1n\log\Nb(n)=\log(1+\sqrt2).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The increment alphabet is $\{0,+1,-1\}$, emitted by $X^{\pm1}$, $Y$, $Y^{-1}$ respectively.
Merge $X$ and $X^{-1}$ into one class: the swap $X\leftrightarrow X^{-1}$ is a bijection of non-backtracking
words preserving $b$-emissions, so the two states generate the same shadow languages.
Non-backtracking then allows, after class $0$, all three symbols; after $+1$, only $\{0,+1\}$; after $-1$,
only $\{0,-1\}$.
The transfer matrix
\[
M=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\ 1&1&0\\ 1&0&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\Nb(n)=\mathbf{1}^{\!\top} M^{\,n-1}\mathbf{u},\quad \mathbf{u}=(1,1,1)^{\!\top},
\]
has characteristic polynomial $(\lambda-1)(\lambda^2-2\lambda-1)$.
Both $\Nb$ and the sequence defined by the Pell recursion with the same initial values satisfy the associated
three-term linear recursion and agree at $n=1,2,3$ ($3,7,17$ by direct computation), hence coincide.
The rate is the Perron root $1+\sqrt2$ of $\lambda^2-2\lambda-1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Generic separation and the branching theorem}
\label{sec:genericity}

\begin{lemma}[Genericity of profile separation]
\label{lem:genericity}
Fix a pre-saturation window $n\le n_{\max}$, meaning $F_k\neq\emptyset$ and $F_k\neq\Zq$ for all $k<n_{\max}$,
and assume $n_{\max}<q/2$ so that $b$-values do not wrap.
Then for every unit probe $\varphi_0$ outside a finite union of proper real-algebraic hypersurfaces, distinct
$b$-shadow sequences induce distinct residual profiles on the window.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Two distinct shadow sequences first differ at some step $k$, with values $b\neq b'$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:b-only}, profile equality at step $k$ reads
$\sum_{m\in A}|\widehat{\varphi_0}(m)|^2=\sum_{m\in B}|\widehat{\varphi_0}(m)|^2$ with
$A=F_{k-1}^{\,c}-cb$ and $B=F_{k-1}^{\,c}-cb'$.
If $A=B$ then $F_{k-1}^{\,c}$ is invariant under the nonzero shift $c(b-b')$; since $q$ is prime, a
shift-invariant subset is $\emptyset$ or $\Zq$, both excluded pre-saturation.
Hence $A\neq B$ and the equality is a nonzero real quadratic form in $\widehat{\varphi_0}$ vanishing; on the
unit sphere its zero set is a proper real-algebraic hypersurface (it is violated by any $\varphi_0$ whose
Fourier mass sits on a frequency in the symmetric difference).
There are finitely many pairs of shadow sequences on a finite window, so the exceptional set is a finite
union of proper hypersurfaces.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Canonical trajectory-branching rate]
\label{thm:branching-rate}
For a generic probe in the sense of Lemma~\ref{lem:genericity}, the canonical O12-compatible D4 channel
satisfies, on the pre-saturation window,
\[
\Ndist(n)=\Nb(n),
\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac1n\log\Ndist(n)=\log(1+\sqrt2).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\Ndist(n)\le\Nb(n)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:b-only}, and $\Ndist(n)\ge\Nb(n)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:genericity}.
The rate is Proposition~\ref{prop:pell}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:witness}
The numerical campaign of Section~\ref{sec:numerics} verifies exact profile separation, at machine precision
and without binning, on all sampled histories at $q=211$: the number of distinct profiles equals the number
of distinct sampled $b$-sequences at every $n\le 12$ (Table~\ref{tab:pell}).
This witnesses that the concrete seeded probe is generic; the lemma guarantees that this is not an accident
of the seed.
\end{remark}

\subsection{V3: full-history compression channel}
\label{sec:v3}

The Gabor channel replaces the O12 span by the span of $\{\rho_c(u)\varphi_0 : u\in B_j\}$, deduplicated by
$(a,b)$.
Its rank grows deterministically as the abelian disk count $2k^2+2k+1$ at every shell and for every block and
character tested, saturating at $k\simeq\sqrt{q/2}$; this is a corollary of the full-spark property of
generic Gabor systems in prime dimension~\cite{Lawrence2005,Malikiosis2015} (Corollary~\ref{cor:gabor-rank}).
Because the basis is not Fourier-aligned, the residual depends on $(a_k,b_k)$; the trivial ceiling is the
word entropy $\log 3$, but the sharp ceiling is $\log 2$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:class-bound}).
The measured rate transiently reaches $0.90$--$0.96$ at $n=3$--$4$ ($q=211$, $\epsilon=10^{-3}$), then
decreases: profiles genuinely merge, and Section~\ref{sec:fullhistory} identifies the mechanism --- geodesic
death --- and pins the asymptotic rate at $\log 2$.
This is authentic projective compression of the full history, not a resolution artefact.
V3 is not used as the canonical channel; its proved compression rate and finite-$n$ profile are retained as
the natural proxy for full-history projective compression and as the possible carrier of an evolving
effective equation of state (Section~\ref{sec:eos}).

\section{The Full-History Channel: Exact Compression Rate}
\label{sec:fullhistory}

This section upgrades the full-history channel V3 of Section~\ref{sec:v3} from a measured compression profile
to an analytic mechanism.
The carrier of the result is a geometric one: the Gabor span exhausts the abelian $\ell^1$ disk shell by
shell, so a trajectory keeps a nonzero symbol exactly as long as its abelian shadow is an $\ell^1$-outward
geodesic, and a single inward step erases the symbol permanently.
The consequence is that the sharp ceiling of the channel is not the word entropy $\log 3$ but $\log 2$, and
that for a generic probe this value is attained: the full-history channel has positive and exactly pinned
symbolic entropy.
The rate is a property of this ball-synchronised Gabor exhaustion, not of the word source.

Throughout, trajectories are identified with their abelian shadows $z_k=(a_k,b_k)\in\mathbb{Z}^2$ taken as
integer points (no wrap: all windows below satisfy $n<q/2$), $|z|_1=|a|+|b|$, and
$D_j=\{(a,b):|a|+|b|\le j\}$ denotes the $\ell^1$ disk, $|D_j|=2j^2+2j+1$.
The V3 symbol of a trajectory at step $k$ is $s_k=r^{(c)}_k\,r^{(q-c)}_k$, where $r^{(c)}_k$ is the residual
norm of $\rho_c(u_k)\varphi_0$ against the Gabor span frozen at the end of shell $k-1$
(Section~\ref{sec:numerics}).

\subsection{Disk structure and geodesic death}
\label{sec:disk-death}

\begin{lemma}[Disk span]
\label{lem:disk-span}
For any probe $\varphi_0$ and any character $c\neq0$, the Gabor span frozen at the end of shell $j$ equals
\[
G_j \;=\; \mathrm{span}\{\rho_c(a,b,0)\,\varphi_0 : (a,b)\in D_j\}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The abelian projection of a length-$\ell$ word has $\ell^1$ norm at most $\ell$, and conversely every
$(a,b)$ with $|a|+|b|\le j$ is the abelian image of a straight word of length $|a|+|b|\le j$, so the
$(a,b)$-deduplicated ball $B_j$ projects exactly onto $D_j$.
The central coordinate contributes only the global phase $\rho_c(a,b,\gamma)=\psi_c(\gamma)\rho_c(a,b,0)$,
which does not affect spans.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Deterministic rank sequence]
\label{cor:gabor-rank}
Since $\rho_c(a,b,0)$ is a global phase times $M^{cb}T^{a}$, the family
$\{\rho_c(a,b,0)\varphi_0\}_{(a,b)\in\Zq^2}$ is the standard finite Gabor system up to a relabelling of
indices.
For $q$ prime and $\varphi_0$ in the dense open full-measure set of~\cite{Lawrence2005}, every subset of at
most $q$ of its members is linearly independent (the Haar property; see also~\cite{Malikiosis2015}), so
\[
\operatorname{rank} G_j=\min\bigl(|D_j|,q\bigr)=\min\bigl(2j^2+2j+1,\;q\bigr).
\]
This is the rank sequence $1,5,13,25,41,\dots$ measured deterministically at every shell in
Section~\ref{sec:v3}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[Geodesic death]
\label{lem:geodesic-death}
For every unit probe, both characters, and every trajectory:
(i) if $|z_k|_1\le k-1$ then $s_k=0$ exactly;
(ii) the deficit $k-|z_k|_1$ is nonnegative, even, and nondecreasing in $k$, so after the first step at
which $|z_k|_1<k$ every subsequent symbol vanishes;
(iii) the symbols up to step $m$ can all be nonzero only if the shadow is an $\ell^1$-outward geodesic,
$|z_k|_1=k$ for all $k\le m$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) If $z_k\in D_{k-1}$ then $\rho_c(u_k)\varphi_0$ is a global phase times one of the vectors spanning
$G_{k-1}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:disk-span}), so both residual factors vanish identically.
(ii) Each step changes $|z|_1$ by $\pm1$, so $|z_k|_1\equiv k \pmod 2$ and the deficit is even; if
$|z_k|_1\le k-2$ then $|z_{k+1}|_1\le k-1$, so the deficit never decreases.
(iii) is (i) and (ii) restated.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Outward geodesic count]
\label{prop:outward-count}
The number $A(m)$ of non-backtracking words of length $m\ge1$ whose shadow is an $\ell^1$-outward geodesic
is
\[
A(m)=2^{m+2}-4 .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An outward step never reverses the previous one, so outwardness already implies non-backtracking.
An outward path stays in the closed quadrant fixed by its axis signs; from an interior point two steps are
outward, from an on-axis point three (the axis step and both transverse steps), and once the path leaves the
axis it never returns (both coordinates can only grow in absolute value).
Conditioning on the number $i\ge0$ of axis steps after the first step,
$A(m)=4\bigl(1+\sum_{i=0}^{m-2}2\cdot2^{m-2-i}\bigr)=2^{m+2}-4$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The unconditional ceiling}
\label{sec:v3-ceiling}

\begin{lemma}[$b$-mirror identity]
\label{lem:b-mirror}
$\rho_{q-c}(a,b,0)=\rho_c(a,-b,0)$ identically on $\mathbb{C}^q$, since $\psi_{q-c}(t)=\psi_c(-t)$.
Consequently the two frozen spans coincide, $G^{(q-c)}_j=G^{(c)}_j$ ($D_j$ is symmetric under
$b\mapsto-b$), the residual factors satisfy $r^{(q-c)}(a,b)=r^{(c)}(a,-b)$, and the pair symbol obeys
\[
s_k(a,b)=r^{(c)}_k(a,b)\;r^{(c)}_k(a,-b)=s_k(a,-b).
\]
Profiles are therefore invariant under the letterwise swap $Y\leftrightarrow Y^{-1}$, for every probe.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proposition}[Unconditional ceiling $\log 2$]
\label{prop:class-bound}
For every unit probe and every horizon $n\ge1$,
\[
\NdistV(n)\;\le\;\frac{\Nclass(n)+2}{2}\;\le\;2^{\,n+2},
\qquad
\Nclass(n)=A(n)+\sum_{d=3}^{n}\bigl(A(d-1)-4\bigr).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The residual factors at step $k$ depend on the path only through $z_k$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:disk-span} and the
global-phase argument), so by Lemma~\ref{lem:geodesic-death} a profile is determined by the outward prefix
up to the first inward step: the death step carries symbol $0$ whichever inward generator realises it, and
everything after it is $0$.
An outward geodesic ending on an axis is a pure $X^{\pm}$ or $Y^{\pm}$ word, and its unique inward
continuation is the backtracking step, which is forbidden; so death at step $d$ requires a non-axis outward
prefix of length $d-1\ge2$, giving $A(d-1)-4$ dead classes for each $3\le d\le n$ and $A(n)$ alive classes.
The involution of Lemma~\ref{lem:b-mirror} identifies these classes in pairs; a fixed class has $b\equiv0$
along its prefix, hence is a pure $X^{\pm}$ class, and these are alive, so exactly $2$ classes are fixed.
Finally $\Nclass(n)\le A(n)+\sum_{m=2}^{n-1}A(m)<2^{n+2}+2^{n+2}$, so
$(\Nclass(n)+2)/2\le2^{\,n+2}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Generic separation and the rate theorem}
\label{sec:v3-rate}

\begin{lemma}[Alive positivity]
\label{lem:alive-positivity}
Let $q$ be prime, $\varphi_0$ in the full-spark set of~\cite{Lawrence2005}, and $n$ such that
$|D_{n-1}|+1\le q$ and $n<q/2$.
Then along every trajectory and for both characters, $|z_k|_1=k$ implies that both residual factors of
$s_k$ are strictly positive, for all $k\le n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The $|D_{k-1}|+1\le q$ vectors $\{\rho_c(w,0)\varphi_0:w\in D_{k-1}\}\cup\{\rho_c(z_k,0)\varphi_0\}$ carry
pairwise distinct index pairs (no wrap for $n<q/2$), hence are linearly independent by the Haar property,
and the residual of the last one against the span of the others is positive.
The $q-c$ channel is the same system by Lemma~\ref{lem:b-mirror}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Generic $|b|$-level separation]
\label{lem:b-level-separation}
Let $q$ be prime and $4n^2\le q$.
Outside a finite union of proper real-algebraic hypersurfaces in probe space, the following holds for every
$k\le n$: if $z\neq z'$ are frontier points at step $k$ (that is, $|z|_1=|z'|_1=k$) with
$|b_z|\neq|b_{z'}|$, then $s_k(z)\neq s_k(z')$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $D=D_{k-1}$ and, for a channel $\chi\in\{c,q-c\}$ and a frontier point $w$, let
$\Delta_\chi(w)=\det\operatorname{Gram}\{\rho_\chi(v,0)\varphi_0:v\in D\cup\{w\}\}$.
On the open set where the two $D$-Grams are invertible, the squared residual is the Gram ratio
$\Delta_\chi(w)/\Delta_\chi(D)$, so $s_k(z)=s_k(z')$ is equivalent there to the vanishing of the real
polynomial
\[
\widetilde P \;=\; \Delta_c(z)\,\Delta_{q-c}(z)\;-\;\Delta_c(z')\,\Delta_{q-c}(z') .
\]
It suffices to prove $\widetilde P\not\equiv0$; the exceptional set is then contained in the zero set of
$\widetilde P$ together with the two Gram-degeneracy hypersurfaces.

Witness.
Let $d=k-|b_z|$ and $d'=k-|b_{z'}|$ and assume without loss of generality $d>d'\ge0$; set $m=2d'+1\le2d-1$.
Choose $s_j=c\,\sigma_j$ with $\sigma_j=(j-1)(2k-1)$ for $j=1,\dots,m$; since
$m(2k-1)\le(2k-1)^2<4n^2\le q$, the frequency sheets $\{cb+s_j:|b|\le k-1\}$ are pairwise disjoint, and so
are their $q-c$ counterparts.
Take the singular window $\varphi_w=m^{-1/2}\sum_j e_{s_j}$.
In Fourier coordinates, $\rho_c(a,b,0)\varphi_w$ is a global phase times $\mathrm{row}_b\otimes v_a$ with
$v_a=m^{-1/2}(\zeta^{-as_1},\dots,\zeta^{-as_m})$, $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/q}$, so the span over $D$ splits into
coordinate blocks indexed by $b$,
\[
\bigoplus_{|b|\le k-1}\ \mathrm{row}_b\otimes C_b,
\qquad
C_b=\mathrm{span}\{v_a : |a|\le k-1-|b|\}.
\]
Any $r\le m$ of the vectors $v_a$ with distinct $a$ are linearly independent, because
$[\zeta^{-as_j}]$ is a minor of the prime-order DFT matrix, and such minors never vanish (Chebotar\"ev's
theorem on roots of unity~\cite{EvansIsaacs1976}; it is also the engine of the full-spark
theorem~\cite{Lawrence2005}).
Hence $\dim C_b=\min(2(k-1-|b|)+1,\,m)$: the column of $z$ satisfies $2d-1\ge m$, so $C_{b_z}=\mathbb{C}^m$
and $r^{(c)}(z)=0$, while the column of $z'$ contributes only $2d'-1<m$ vectors, so together with
$v_{a_{z'}}$ they stay independent and $r^{(c)}(z')>0$ (for $d'=0$ the block of $z'$ is empty and the
residual is full).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:b-mirror} the same holds in the $q-c$ channel, so the witness gives $s_k(z)=0<s_k(z')$.

Perturbation.
At $\varphi_w$ the $D$-Grams are singular, so we evaluate $\widetilde P$ along $\varphi_t=\varphi_w+t\Psi$.
If an affine family of $N\le q$ vectors has rank $r$ at $t=0$, then
$\det\operatorname{Gram}=\|v_1(t)\wedge\dots\wedge v_N(t)\|^2=t^{2(N-r)}\bigl(c(\Psi)+O(t)\bigr)$ with
$c(\Psi)$ the squared norm of a mixed wedge, nonzero for generic $\Psi$.
At the witness, in each channel $\operatorname{rank}(D\cup z)=\operatorname{rank}(D)=:R$ while
$\operatorname{rank}(D\cup z')=R+1$, so $\Delta_\chi(z)$ vanishes to order $2(|D|+1-R)$ and
$\Delta_\chi(z')$ to order $2(|D|-R)$: the $z'$ product dominates by four orders, and for generic $\Psi$
\[
\widetilde P(\varphi_t)=-c_1(\Psi)c_2(\Psi)\,t^{\,4(|D|-R)}+O\bigl(t^{\,4(|D|-R)+1}\bigr)\neq0
\]
for all small $t\neq0$.
Hence $\widetilde P\not\equiv0$.
There are finitely many steps $k\le n$ and level pairs, so the union of the exceptional sets is a finite
union of proper hypersurfaces.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full-history rate]
\label{thm:v3-rate}
Let $q$ be prime, $4n^2\le q$, and let the probe avoid the finite union of the full-spark exceptional
set~\cite{Lawrence2005} and the hypersurfaces of Lemma~\ref{lem:b-level-separation}
(a dense open full-measure condition).
Then
\[
2^{\,n}\;\le\;\NdistV(n)\;\le\;\frac{\Nclass(n)+2}{2}\;\le\;2^{\,n+2},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac1n\log\NdistV(n)=\log 2
\]
along any family with $q\ge4n^2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The upper bound is Proposition~\ref{prop:class-bound}.
For the lower bound, map each pattern $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}^n$ to the positive-quadrant staircase that
takes $Y$ at step $k$ when $\varepsilon_k=1$ and $X$ otherwise; these are alive outward geodesics with
$b_k=\sum_{i\le k}\varepsilon_i$.
Two distinct patterns first differ at some step $k$, where the two staircases sit at frontier points with
distinct $|b|$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:b-level-separation} their symbols at step $k$ differ, while their
profiles agree before $k$, so the $2^n$ profiles are pairwise distinct.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Strict compression]
\label{cor:strict-compression}
On its admissible window the full-history channel carries positive and exactly pinned symbolic entropy,
strictly below the canonical channel and the word source:
\[
0\;<\;h^{\mathrm{V3}}=\log 2\;<\;\log(1+\sqrt2)=\hb\;<\;\log 3 .
\]
Projective compression of the full history is therefore no longer only observed
(Section~\ref{sec:v3}); it has an analytic rate, and the finer Gabor resolution strictly lowers the
distinguishable-history entropy relative to the canonical $b$-shadow channel.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Generic $a$-mirror separation: the mixed two-anchor coefficient}
\label{sec:amirror}

This subsection closes the analytic core of the missing step named above: for a frontier pair
$z=(a,b)$, $z'=(-a,b)$ with $|a|+|b|=k$ and $a\neq0$, the fusion $s_k(z)=s_k(z')$ is confined to a
proper subset of probe space.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:b-mirror} we may take $a>0$ and $b\ge0$, and write $F(\alpha,\beta)$ for the Gram
determinant $\Delta_c$ of the tested system $D_{k-1}\cup\{(\alpha,\beta)\}$ in the $c$ channel, so
that on the open set of invertible $D$-Grams the fusion is equivalent to the vanishing of the real
polynomial
\[
\widetilde P \;=\; F(a,b)\,F(a,-b)\;-\;F(-a,b)\,F(-a,-b).
\]

The witness family is a weighted sparse probe with two satellites.
Fix $m$ anchor positions $A_1,\dots,A_m\in\Zq$ whose pairwise circular distances exceed $2k+2$,
weights $w_j>0$, satellite amplitudes $x_1,x_2\in\mathbb{C}$, and set
\[
\widehat\varphi
\;=\;
\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sqrt{w_j}\,e_{cA_j}
\;+\;
i\epsilon\bigl(x_1 e_{c(A_1+1)}+x_2 e_{c(A_2+1)}\bigr).
\]
In Fourier coordinates $\rho_c(\alpha,\beta,0)$ shifts the support by $c\beta$, so the Gram of a
tested system splits into frequency sheets indexed by $\beta$: at $\epsilon=0$ it is block diagonal,
with sheet block $A_\beta=W_\beta W_\beta^\ast$,
$W_\beta[\alpha,j]=\sqrt{w_j}\,\zeta^{c(A_j+\beta)\alpha}$, rows indexed by
$\Lambda_\beta=\{\alpha:|\alpha|+|\beta|\le k-1\}$, augmented by $\alpha=a$ on the sheet $\beta=b$
of the tested point.
Since $|a|=k-|b|$, the augmented set is again a consecutive integer range, so every sheet block is a
weighted minor of the prime-order DFT and is invertible whenever $m\ge|\Lambda_\beta|$
(Chebotar\"ev~\cite{EvansIsaacs1976}, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:b-level-separation}).
The anchor separation kills every support overlap except the two anchor--satellite edges, so the
order-$\epsilon$ block $B$ couples adjacent sheets only.
For a sheet pair $(\beta,\beta+1)$ set $\tau_j=c(A_j+1+\beta)$ and
$w_\beta(t)=(\zeta^{t\alpha})_{\alpha\in\Lambda_\beta}$.

\begin{proposition}[Mixed two-anchor coefficient]
\label{prop:mixed-two-anchor-coefficient}
With the notation above, and all sheet blocks invertible,
\[
\widetilde P
\;=\;
F_0(b)\,F_0(-b)\;4\epsilon^2\sqrt{w_1w_2}\,\Im(x_1\overline{x_2})\;S(a,b)
\;+\;O(\epsilon^3),
\]
where $F_0(\pm b)$ are the $\epsilon=0$ determinants,
\[
S(a,b)
\;=\;
\bigl[\Im Q_{b-1}+\Im Q_b\bigr]_{\mathrm{sys}(a,b)}
\;+\;
\bigl[\Im Q_{-b-1}+\Im Q_{-b}\bigr]_{\mathrm{sys}(a,-b)},
\]
\[
Q_\beta
\;=\;
K_\beta(\tau_1,\tau_2)\,K_{\beta+1}(\tau_2,\tau_1),
\qquad
K_\beta(s,t)
\;=\;
w_\beta(s)^\ast A_\beta^{-1}w_\beta(t),
\]
the sums running over the sheet pairs adjacent to the tested sheet (empty sheets omitted; for $b=0$
the two systems coincide and each pair is counted twice).
In particular the first $a$-odd contribution is purely mixed in the two satellite amplitudes.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Sheetwise, the tested Gram is $G(\epsilon)=A+\epsilon B+\epsilon^2 C$ with $A$ block diagonal, $B$
supported on adjacent sheet pairs through the two edges (entries proportional to
$i\sqrt{w_j}\,x_j$ and $-i\sqrt{w_j}\,\overline{x_j}$), and $C$ same-sheet with entries proportional
to $|x_j|^2$.
Since $B$ is block off-diagonal, $\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}B)=0$ and the order-$\epsilon$ term
vanishes; the determinant expansion gives
\[
\det G(\epsilon)
=
\det A\,
\Bigl(
1+\epsilon^2\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}C)
-\tfrac{\epsilon^2}{2}\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}BA^{-1}B)
\Bigr)
+O(\epsilon^3).
\]
The antiunitary $K\varphi(x)=\overline{\varphi(-x)}$ satisfies
$K\rho_c(\alpha,\beta,0)K=\rho_c(-\alpha,\beta,0)$ and acts on the family by
$(x_1,x_2)\mapsto(-\overline{x_1},-\overline{x_2})$, fixing the $\epsilon=0$ probe; identifying the
bilinear parts of the two expansions gives $Q_\beta(-a)=\overline{Q_\beta(a)}$ and shows that every
coefficient of $|x_j|^2$ is $a$-even: the $C$-traces are real (traces of $A^{-1}$ against positive
semidefinite dyads), as are the same-satellite loop terms $|x_j|^2K_\beta(\tau_j,\tau_j)
K_{\beta+1}(\tau_j,\tau_j)$, so both are invariant under $a\mapsto-a$ and cancel in $\widetilde P$.
The only surviving order-$\epsilon^2$ contribution to the $a$-odd part is the mixed bilinear term of
$\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}BA^{-1}B)$; writing $B$ as a sum of dyads over the two edges reduces each
adjacent-sheet trace to the displayed kernel products, with
$\sqrt{w_1w_2}\,x_1\overline{x_2}$ in front, and $a$ enters only through the two sheet pairs
adjacent to the tested sheet.
Finally $Q_\beta(-a)=\overline{Q_\beta(a)}$ turns the mirror difference into
$2i\,\Im Q_\beta$, and collecting the pairs of both tested systems gives $S(a,b)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Moment reduction of the mixed coefficient]
\label{lem:mixed-coefficient-moment-reduction}
Let $\xi_j=\zeta^{cA_j}$, $\mu=\zeta^{c}$, and $p(d)=\sum_j w_j\xi_j^{\,d}$.
Then $A_\beta=D_\beta P_{\Lambda_\beta}D_\beta^\ast$ with $D_\beta=
\operatorname{diag}(\zeta^{c\beta\alpha})$ and $P_\Lambda[\alpha,\alpha']=p(\alpha-\alpha')$, the
sheet phases cancel inside the kernels, and $S(a,b)$ depends on the anchor configuration only
through the moments $p(d)$ and the pair $(\xi_1,\xi_2)$:
\begin{align*}
S
=
\Im\Bigl[\,
&k_{C_1}(\xi_1\mu,\xi_2\mu)\,k_{Z}(\xi_2,\xi_1)
+k_{Z}(\xi_1\mu,\xi_2\mu)\,k_{C_2}(\xi_2,\xi_1)\\
{}+{}&k_{C_2}(\xi_1\mu,\xi_2\mu)\,k_{Z}(\xi_2,\xi_1)
+k_{Z}(\xi_1\mu,\xi_2\mu)\,k_{C_1}(\xi_2,\xi_1)
\Bigr],
\end{align*}
where $k_\Lambda(\nu,\nu')=\widehat w(\nu)^\ast P_\Lambda^{-1}\widehat w(\nu')$,
$\widehat w(\nu)_\alpha=\nu^{\alpha}$, and, with $\ell=k-1-|b|$, the ranges are
$C_1=\{-(\ell+1),\dots,\ell+1\}$, $Z=\{-\ell,\dots,\ell+1\}$,
$C_2=\{-(\ell-1),\dots,\ell-1\}$ for $b\ge1$
(for $b=0$: $C_{1,2}$ replaced by $\{-(k-2),\dots,k-2\}$, $Z=\{-(k-1),\dots,k\}$, pairs doubled).
In particular $S$ is a real-analytic function of $(p(d))_d$ on the domain where all sheet Toeplitz
matrices are positive definite.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Conjugating each sheet block by $D_\beta$ replaces $A_\beta$ by the Toeplitz matrix
$P_{\Lambda_\beta}$ and the kernel vectors by $\widehat w(\xi_j\mu)$ on the lower sheet of a pair
and $\widehat w(\xi_j)$ on the upper sheet: the $\beta$-dependence cancels, leaving only the ranges
and the $\mu$-dressing of the lower slot.
Enumerating the four sheet pairs of Proposition~\ref{prop:mixed-two-anchor-coefficient} gives the
displayed form; rationality of $P_\Lambda^{-1}$ in the entries gives real-analyticity on the
positive-definite domain.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Real span of anchor moment jets]
\label{lem:anchor-moment-real-span}
Let $D\ge1$ and let $\xi_1,\dots,\xi_m$ be distinct points on the unit circle with $m\ge2D+1$.
Then the real-linear map
\[
\mathbb{R}^m\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{D},
\qquad
w\longmapsto\Bigl(p(0),\,\bigl(p(d)\bigr)_{d=1,\dots,D}\Bigr),
\qquad
p(d)=\sum_j w_j\xi_j^{\,d},
\]
is surjective; consequently the image of the open cone $\{w:w_j>0\}$ is open.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If the map were not surjective, a nonzero real-linear functional would annihilate its image: there
would exist $(c_0,h)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{D}$, not all zero, with
$c_0\,p(0)+\Re\sum_{d=1}^{D}\overline{h_d}\,p(d)=0$ for every $w$, hence
$c_0+\Re\sum_d\overline{h_d}\,\xi_j^{\,d}=0$ for every $j$.
The left-hand side is a nonzero real trigonometric polynomial of degree at most $D$, which has at
most $2D$ zeros on the circle, contradicting $m\ge2D+1$.
A surjective linear map is open.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Hermitian jet at the orthogonal moment]
\label{lem:orthogonal-moment-hermitian-jet}
Normalise $p(0)=1$ and let $p_0$ be the orthogonal moment point, $p_0(d)=\delta_{d0}$.
Then $S=0$ at $p_0$.
Along the Hermitian direction that perturbs the single mode $f$ ($p(f)=t$, $p(-f)=\overline t$, all
other off-diagonal moments zero), the first variation is $\mathrm{d}S=2\Re(A\,t)$ with, for
$b\ge1$, $L=k-|b|=|a|$ and $f=2L$,
\[
A
=
\sin(L\theta_0)\,\bigl(\mu^{-2L}-1\bigr)\,(\xi_1\xi_2)^{-L},
\qquad
\theta_0=\arg\bigl(\overline{\xi_1}\xi_2\bigr),
\]
and, for $b=0$ and $f=2k-1$,
\[
A
=
i\,D_{k-2}(\theta_0)\,\bigl(\mu^{-(2k-1)}-1\bigr)\,(\xi_1\xi_2)^{-k}\,(\xi_2-\xi_1),
\qquad
D_r(\theta)=\sum_{|\alpha|\le r}e^{i\alpha\theta}.
\]
For $q$ prime and $A_1\neq A_2$ these jets are nonzero for every admissible anchor pair: each
condition $\sin(L\theta_0)\neq0$, $D_{k-2}(\theta_0)\neq0$, $\mu^{f}\neq1$, $\xi_1\neq\xi_2$ reads
$u\,c\,(A_2-A_1)\not\equiv0 \pmod q$ or $u\,c\not\equiv0\pmod q$ for some $0<u<q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At $p_0$ every $P_\Lambda$ is the identity, so $k_\Lambda(\nu,\nu')=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda}
\rho^{\alpha}$ with $\rho=\overline{\nu}\nu'$: the $\mu$-dressing cancels in $\rho$, the four
products of Lemma~\ref{lem:mixed-coefficient-moment-reduction} pair into conjugates, and $S=0$.
Linearising $P_\Lambda^{-1}=I-tE_f-\overline t E_{-f}+O(|t|^2)$, where $E_{\pm f}$ is the
$0$/$1$ matrix of the $\pm f$ diagonal, only the range of largest spread carries the mode $f$, and
it does so on its single corner pair: for $b\ge1$ this is $C_1$, with
$\widehat w(\nu)^\ast E_f\widehat w(\nu')=(\nu\nu')^{-L}$, so
$\delta k_{C_1}(\nu,\nu')=-t(\nu\nu')^{-L}-\overline t(\nu\nu')^{L}$, and only the first and fourth
terms contribute:
\[
\delta S
=
\Im\bigl[\delta k_{C_1}(\xi_1\mu,\xi_2\mu)\bigl(D_\ell(\theta_0)+\overline\rho^{\,L}\bigr)
+\bigl(D_\ell(\theta_0)+\rho^{L}\bigr)\,\delta k_{C_1}(\xi_2,\xi_1)\bigr].
\]
Writing $g=\xi_1\xi_2$ and collecting the $\overline t$ terms as conjugates of $t$ terms, the
bracket reduces to $t\,g^{-L}\bigl(\mu^{-2L}-1\bigr)\bigl(\rho^{L}-\overline\rho^{\,L}\bigr)$, that
is $\mathrm{d}S=2\Re(A\,t)$ with the displayed $A$.
For $b=0$ the mode $f=2k-1$ lives on the corner pair of $Z$,
$\widehat w(\nu)^\ast E_f\widehat w(\nu')=\nu^{-k}\nu'^{-(k-1)}$, both doubled pairs contribute
with the real factor $D_{k-2}(\theta_0)$, and the same collection gives the displayed $A$ through
$\xi_2^{-(k-1)}\xi_1^{-k}-\xi_2^{-k}\xi_1^{-(k-1)}=g^{-k}(\xi_2-\xi_1)$.
Each nonvanishing condition is of the stated $\mathbb{F}_q^{\times}$ form because
$\sin(L\theta_0)=0$ iff $2Lc(A_2-A_1)\equiv0$, $D_{k-2}(\theta_0)=0$ iff
$(2k-3)c(A_2-A_1)\equiv0$, and $\mu^{f}=1$ iff $fc\equiv0\pmod q$, with every factor a nonzero
residue.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Generic non-vanishing of the mixed coefficient]
\label{thm:generic-nonvanishing-mixed-coefficient}
Let $q$ be prime, $2\le k$, $q>(4k-1)(2k+3)$, and let $(a,b)$ be a frontier pair with $a\neq0$.
Then $S(a,b)$ is not the zero function on the positive Toeplitz moment domain, and its zero set is
a proper closed real-analytic subset.
Consequently there exist admissible weighted anchor configurations with $S(a,b)\neq0$, the
polynomial $\widetilde P$ is not identically zero on probe space, and the $a$-mirror fusion locus
$\{s_k(a,b)=s_k(-a,b)\}$ is contained in a proper real-algebraic subset of probe space.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Set $D=2k-1$ (the largest sheet spread) and $m=4k-1\ge2D+1$; the window $q>(4k-1)(2k+3)$ leaves
room for $m$ anchor positions with pairwise circular distances exceeding $2k+2$.
Let $\Omega$ be the set of moment vectors $\bigl(p(0),(p(d))_{d\le D}\bigr)$ for which every sheet
Toeplitz matrix is positive definite: $\Omega$ is open, convex (the constraints are linear in $p$),
hence connected, and contains the orthogonal point $p_0$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:mixed-coefficient-moment-reduction}, $S$ is real-analytic on $\Omega$.
For every weight vector $w>0$ the moment vector $p(w)$ lies in $\Omega$, since each sheet Toeplitz
matrix is the weighted Gram of distinct DFT columns (Chebotar\"ev~\cite{EvansIsaacs1976}); by
Lemma~\ref{lem:anchor-moment-real-span} the image $U=\{p(w):w>0\}$ is a nonempty open subset of
$\Omega$.
If $S$ vanished identically on $U$, real-analytic continuation on the connected set $\Omega$ would
force $S\equiv0$ on $\Omega$, contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:orthogonal-moment-hermitian-jet}: the
germ of $S$ at $p_0\in\Omega$ is nonzero, because its first variation along an admissible Hermitian
direction is $2\Re(A\,t)\not\equiv0$.
Hence $S\neq0$ at some $p(w^\ast)$, $w^\ast>0$, and the zero set of $S$ is a proper closed
real-analytic subset of $\Omega$.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:mixed-two-anchor-coefficient} the $\epsilon^2$ coefficient of
$\widetilde P$ along the witness family at $w^\ast$ is nonzero whenever $\Im(x_1\overline{x_2})
\neq0$, so $\widetilde P\not\equiv0$ as a polynomial on probe space, and the fusion locus is
contained in the union of its zero set with the Gram degeneracy hypersurfaces.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The exact class count as a theorem}
\label{sec:v3-count-theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:generic-nonvanishing-mixed-coefficient} closes the analytic half of the exact count; the
remaining half is combinatorial, and rests on a rigidity property of outward geodesics.

\begin{lemma}[Separation of quotient classes]
\label{lem:v3-quotient-class-separation}
Let $q$ be prime and $n\ge2$ with $q>(4n-1)(2n+3)$.
Outside a finite union of proper real-algebraic hypersurfaces in probe space, two full-history classes of
Proposition~\ref{prop:class-bound} have the same V3 profile up to depth $n$ if and only if they are
identified by the $b$-mirror involution of Lemma~\ref{lem:b-mirror}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
First, $q>(4n-1)(2n+3)$ implies $4n^2\le q$, so Lemmas~\ref{lem:alive-positivity}
and~\ref{lem:b-level-separation} apply on the whole horizon.
A profile is determined by the outward prefix up to the first inward step
(Proposition~\ref{prop:class-bound}), so classes are outward prefixes: of length $n$ for alive classes,
with a marked death step $d\le n$ and length $d-1$ for dead ones.
If one class is alive at a step $d$ at which the other is dead, Lemma~\ref{lem:alive-positivity} separates
the profiles there ($s_d>0$ against $s_d=0$); two dead classes with distinct death steps are separated at
the earlier one.
It therefore suffices to separate two distinct outward prefixes over a common horizon $h\ge2$, with $h=n$
for alive classes and $h=d-1\ge2$ for a common death step $d$.
If $|b_k|\neq|b'_k|$ at some $k\le h$, Lemma~\ref{lem:b-level-separation} separates the profiles at the
first such $k$.
Otherwise $|b_k|=|b'_k|$, hence $|a_k|=|a'_k|$, for every $k\le h$.
Along an outward geodesic every step increases $|a|$ or $|b|$ by one, so $|a_k|$ and $|b_k|$ are
non-decreasing, a coordinate never returns to zero once it has left it, and its sign is the sign of its
first step.
The unsigned sequences $(|a_k|,|b_k|)_{k\le h}$ therefore determine the prefix up to the two global signs:
the two prefixes agree up to the Klein group generated by the $a$-mirror and the $b$-mirror.
Modulo the forced $b$-mirror the only nontrivial case is the pointwise $a$-mirror, $z'_k=(-a_k,b_k)$ for
all $k$, and the two classes are distinct precisely when the prefix contains an $a$-step.
In that case $a_h\neq0$ (dead prefixes are non-axis, so they contain an $a$-step and $a_{d-1}\neq0$), the
points $(a_h,b_h)$ and $(-a_h,b_h)$ form a frontier pair with nonzero $a$-coordinate, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:generic-nonvanishing-mixed-coefficient}, applied at step $k=h$ (its window
$q>(4h-1)(2h+3)$ is implied), confines their fusion to a proper real-algebraic subset: outside it the two
profiles differ at step $h$.
There are finitely many class pairs and steps, so the union of all exceptional sets remains a finite union
of proper real-algebraic hypersurfaces.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact full-history class count]
\label{thm:v3-exact-class-count}
Let $q$ be prime and $n\ge2$ with $q>(4n-1)(2n+3)$.
For every probe outside a finite union of proper real-algebraic hypersurfaces,
\[
\NdistV(n)
\;=\;
\frac{\Nclass(n)+2}{2}
\;=\;
2^{\,n+2}-4n-1 .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:class-bound} gives $\NdistV(n)\le(\Nclass(n)+2)/2$, the factor two being the forced
$b$-mirror identification, whose only fixed classes are the two pure $X^{\pm}$ classes.
Lemma~\ref{lem:v3-quotient-class-separation} gives the reverse inequality generically: after quotienting by
the $b$-mirror, no two distinct classes share a profile.
The closed form follows from $A(m)=2^{m+2}-4$:
\[
\Nclass(n)=A(n)+\sum_{d=3}^{n}\bigl(A(d-1)-4\bigr)=2^{\,n+3}-8n-4,
\qquad
\frac{\Nclass(n)+2}{2}=2^{\,n+2}-4n-1 .
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact symbolic entropy from the exact count]
\label{cor:v3-exact-symbolic-entropy}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-exact-class-count},
$\tfrac1n\log\NdistV(n)\to\log2$ along every prime family with $q>(4n-1)(2n+3)$: the rate of
Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-rate} is recovered with the exact count in place of the bracket.
\end{corollary}

At $n=1$ the count remains $2$ rather than $(\Nclass(1)+2)/2=3$, as noted in
Section~\ref{sec:v3-count}: the two $X^{\pm}$ classes merge exactly at the first step, so the theorem
starts at $n=2$.

\section{Cosmological Interpretation}
\label{sec:cosmology}

\subsection{The corrected transfer from LPS to Heisenberg}
\label{sec:transfer}

The transfer from LPS to Heisenberg changes the location of exponential growth: it is absent from endpoint
volumes and present in projectively distinguishable histories.

\begin{enumerate}
\item Heisenberg endpoint volume is polynomial (Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoint-obstruction}), so endpoint
counting cannot drive acceleration.
\item Non-backtracking histories grow exponentially even on polynomial-growth groups: the redundancy
$3^n/n^4$ is structural.
\item Endpoint-based history compression collapses to the Bass--Guivarc'h no-go
(Corollary~\ref{cor:endpoint-nogo}).
\item Projective D4 distinguishability escapes the endpoint no-go, and the canonical O12-compatible channel
gives Pell growth with $\hb=\log(1+\sqrt2)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:branching-rate}).
\item The full-history channel is strictly compressive with exactly pinned rate $\log 2<\hb$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-rate}); its finite-$n$ compression profile is the candidate for an evolving effective
equation of state (Sections~\ref{sec:fullhistory} and~\ref{sec:eos}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Relation to the de Sitter limit}
\label{sec:desitter}

The projective de Sitter reading is not that matter sources a negative-pressure substance.
It is that the admissible projection process carries a positive distinguishable-history entropy rate, so the
number of admissible reprojection histories grows exponentially independently of any material content ---
the structural analogue of expansion as a property of the dynamics itself rather than of a
source term~\cite{deSitter1917}.
In this reading, the de Sitter-like component is the asymptotic rate of projective history branching, and
the early window ($n\le2$, where profiles are still projectively degenerate) reproduces the decelerated
regime of the two-regime model of~\cite{Beau2026a5} before the branching regime takes over.

\subsection{Status of the equation of state}
\label{sec:eos}

The canonical $b$-shadow rate is rigid: it is a substrate-combinatorial constant, independent of $q$ and of
the spectral data, and therefore gives a clean positive asymptotic rate but not, by itself, an evolving
equation of state.
The evolving component, if present, must come from full-history compression, represented here by V3.
Its asymptotic rate is now pinned analytically ($\log 2$, Theorem~\ref{thm:v3-rate}) and its finite-$n$
count is bracketed in closed form, so the candidate is analytic rather than purely numerical; its
cosmological use remains conditional on a canonical identification of the full-history projective
observable.
Any confrontation with evolving-dark-energy indications~\cite{DESI2024} therefore belongs to the cosmological
branch~\cite{Beau2026d} and only after that identification; it is not claimed here.

\section{Neighbouring Theory}
\label{sec:neighbouring}

\subsection{Non-backtracking entropy and Ihara zeta}
The Pell count is adjacent to non-backtracking path combinatorics as organised by the Ihara zeta
function~\cite{Ihara1966}.
The relevant object here is not the growth of closed geodesics or of the group, but the growth of
non-backtracking words modulo a projection channel; the transfer-matrix mechanics are the same, the quotient
is new.

\subsection{Growth of groups}
The endpoint obstruction is a direct consequence of polynomial growth of nilpotent
groups~\cite{Bass1972,Guivarch1973}.
The result does not conflict with Bass--Guivarc'h theory; it exploits precisely the separation between
endpoint growth and path growth.

\subsection{Finite Gabor systems}
The full-history channel is a finite Weyl--Heisenberg (Gabor) system.
The deterministic rank sequence $1,5,13,25,41,61,\dots,q$ observed at every shell matches the abelian disk
count $2k^2+2k+1$, consistent with the generic linear independence of Gabor systems in prime
dimension~\cite{Lawrence2005} and its extensions~\cite{Malikiosis2015}.
Here this is no longer only consistency: the rank sequence is a corollary of the full-spark property
(Corollary~\ref{cor:gabor-rank}), and the same circle of ideas --- Chebotar\"ev's theorem on the minors of
the prime-order DFT matrix~\cite{EvansIsaacs1976} --- powers the generic separation lemma behind the exact
compression rate (Lemma~\ref{lem:b-level-separation}).

\subsection{Causal sets and history counting}
The reading ``growth is carried by histories rather than states'' is adjacent to causal-set and sum-over-
histories intuitions~\cite{Bombelli1987}.
The distinction is that the present framework counts histories only through projective distinguishability
under a non-injective projection~\cite{Cosmochrony,Beau2026b}, not as independent ontological paths.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The Heisenberg transition does not kill the cosmological branching mechanism; it relocates it.
The endpoint space has polynomial growth, but the projectively distinguishable history space retains a
positive entropy rate.
For the canonical O12-compatible residual channel this rate is pinned exactly:
\[
\Ndist(n)=\Nb(n)=2\Nb(n-1)+\Nb(n-2),
\qquad
\hb=\log(1+\sqrt2),
\]
proved for a generic probe and witnessed exactly on the sampled multi-prime campaign.
This supplies the structural bridge from the spectral admissibility cascade to a de Sitter-like asymptotic
component independent of matter content.
The full-history channel is now itself analytic and exactly counted: geodesic death pins its symbolic
entropy at $\log 2$, strictly below the canonical rate, and the class count is the theorem
$\NdistV(n)=2^{n+2}-4n-1$ --- the $b$-mirror is the only generic degeneracy of the projective history
record, the $a$-mirror being broken by the mixed two-anchor coefficient.
Its finite-$n$ compression profile remains the candidate mechanism for an evolving effective equation of
state; that reading stays interpretive and is deferred to the cosmological branch.

\subsection*{Interpretive outlook}
The following reading is interpretive: a structural interpretation of the proved statements above, not
itself a derived result.
Within the programme, this note says that what grows exponentially is not the set of states of the
substrate --- endpoint growth is polynomial --- but the set of histories that the non-injective projection
can distinguish: accelerated expansion, if inherited by the cosmological branch, is a property of the
projective record, available over a polynomially growing state space and independent of matter content.
The exact class count sharpens the reading: the branching skeleton of the distinguishable-history space is
completely explicit --- outward geodesic prefixes, identified only under one exact mirror symmetry --- so
the de Sitter-like component rests on an enumerable combinatorial object rather than on an asymptotic
estimate, and any future cosmological use inherits a discrete, falsifiable signature.
A third party should situate the claim as conditional on two open steps stated in
Section~\ref{sec:status}: the dictionary between shell rank and cosmic time, and the transfer of the
admissibility cascade to the cosmological branch.
